As part of Druk.help’s ongoing visit to Bhutan, the team held two important meetings today with leaders from the health and disability sectors. Both conversations focused on how digital tools can strengthen existing systems, support frontline teams, and ultimately create solutions that can be sustained within Bhutan.



      

## Meeting with the Health Director, GMC



      

This morning, **Ranjit Balaji, Phub Zam, and Karma Wangchuk** met with **Health Director Tashi Penjor of GMC**, together with his team at Gikuna. The team presented its work and demonstrated the tools it has been developing.



      

The Director responded very positively to the presentation and live demonstration. He asked the team to prepare a **detailed proposal outlining a sustainable commercial model**, with a clear value proposition for GMC and the practical details needed to support its implementation.



      

He also emphasized that **GMC is open to supporting ideas that can be sustainably integrated within the organization**. While a nationwide rollout would eventually require approval from Govt.Tech, he highlighted GMC as an appropriate place to **pilot and validate the solution before considering a national scale-up**.



      

### Building with Data Sovereignty in Mind



      

Data ownership and sovereignty were also important parts of the discussion. Ranjit Balaji clarified that the data and infrastructure would remain on **Govt.Tech servers**, while Druk.help’s role would be limited to supporting the tools and responding to any tool-related outages or technical issues.



      

To demonstrate what this could look like at scale, Ranjit Balaji also showcased the **Kerala.care dashboard** as a reference. The platform is government-owned, with data structured in a way that can support policymakers in understanding needs and making informed decisions.



      

The discussion also expanded beyond a single healthcare application. Ranjit Balaji shared the broader vision of building **seven interconnected grids**, beginning with healthcare, with the aim of creating digital public infrastructure that can support different areas of public service.



      

The meeting ended with a strong sense of optimism and an invitation to explore how the tools could be developed into a sustainable model within GMC.

## Meeting with DPOB



      

At 2 PM, the team met with **Dr. Kinley Wangchuk, Executive Director of DPOB**, joined by AD Pelsung, Chandra, Varun, and Ranjit Balaji.



      

AD Karma began by providing context about the visit, after which Phub and Rinzin Seldon presented and demonstrated the tools.



      

Dr. Kinley then shared more about DPOB’s work supporting **persons with disabilities**. He explained how DPOB provides financial assistance through donation-based funds, retaining a portion for operations and distributing the remaining funds to support the daily needs of people with disabilities.



      

The conversation then moved toward how technology could strengthen this work.



      

Varun explained the **token system** used to recognize and incentivize volunteers for their contributions. Ranjit Balaji followed by giving an overview of how the tools were developed, including the backend architecture and the experience of scaling similar systems to serve **20 crore people in India**.



      

He also shared an update on the **palliative care dashboard going live in India**, demonstrating how the same approach to digital infrastructure can support different areas of public service.



      

### A Potential Solution for Nationwide Registration



      

The ED responded enthusiastically to the possibilities. One of the key challenges currently facing DPOB is the effort to **identify and register persons with disabilities across the country**. This becomes particularly difficult during the monsoon season, when reaching communities and conducting field registrations can be challenging.



      

The tools demonstrated could potentially help DPOB **speed up this registration process, organize information more efficiently, and strengthen the way teams work in the field**.



      

The meeting concluded on an optimistic note, with strong interest in exploring how these tools could be integrated into DPOB’s ongoing work.

## From Conversations to Possibilities



      

Both meetings highlighted a common theme: **technology is most valuable when it strengthens the systems and people already working on the ground**.



      

From healthcare to disability support, the conversations showed how digital tools can help organizations improve coordination, make better use of data, and reach more people — while keeping ownership and infrastructure within Bhutan’s public systems.



      

These discussions are an important step toward understanding what it would take to build solutions that are not only useful today, but can also be **sustained, adapted, and scaled within Bhutan over the long term**.
