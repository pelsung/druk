## What we saw

This morning, Mentor Ranjit Balaji, Thinley Dorji, and Rinzin Seldon met with Mr. Sonam Wangchuk, Program Officer of the Cancer Control Program, to learn about the current state of palliative care in Bhutan. They learned that services are already available in 15 districts, started by focal persons at the district hospitals, and that the teams in these districts have received the training they need to serve their communities. To extend care to the districts not yet covered, Bhutan will need a stronger network of volunteers and caregivers who can support community outreach.

## Personal reflection

**Why did we come for the meeting?** We went to the meeting with three goals. First, we wanted to share our mission with Mr. Sonam Wangchuk and explain what Druk help hopes to contribute. Second, we wanted to gain insight into the palliative care unit and the programs it runs across the country. Third, and most important, we wanted to seek a partnership with the program, because we believe any tool we build will only be useful if it grows alongside the people who deliver care. We came away with a much clearer picture of where palliative care stands in Bhutan today and in what other areas our team would be able to contribute further.

**What happened?** We were also told about an upcoming pilot palliative care training program, expected to begin in the first week of November across five dzongkhags (Thimphu, Punakha, Gasa, Bumthang and Pemagatshel ) .Additionally, a two-day basic palliative care training course will be provided to us prior to the pilot training programs. We saw this event as an important step for us. Understanding the fundamentals of palliative care will help us engage more meaningfully with healthcare professionals and make sure that what we build is grounded in the real needs of patients, caregivers, volunteers, and healthcare teams. It could be a real chance for us to travel to the selected places, demonstrate our digital tools, learn directly from the healthcare teams, and share our own knowledge and experience as part of the training.

**What’s next?** The discussions reminded us that expanding palliative care is not only about building technology. It is about strengthening people, knowledge, and systems that make care possible. We are looking forward to the training and the district visits, where we can keep learning from the field and explore how digital tools can help make palliative care more accessible across Bhutan. We realized how much we still have to learn before we can build anything useful.
