## What we saw

To understand the reality on the ground, we began with field visits: the Palliative Care Unit led the first day, and the Royal Society for Senior Citizens led the second. We visited a total of 5 different home care patients. The visits helped us gain practical knowledge and experience in understanding the needs of home care receivers. It also helped us identify challenges and think of practical and innovative solutions to improve their physical and emotional well-being.

## Personal reflection

**Why did we come for the meeting?** We visited care receivers to understand the ground realities and ensure that our proposed solutions would be built on practical needs and real-life challenges. However, we did not expect the field visits to be so emotionally overwhelming. Witnessing the difficulties faced by care receivers left many of us heartbroken, with some of us moved to tears.

**What happened?** The experience made us realise that although technology has great potential to improve healthcare services, it cannot, on its own, address all the challenges care receivers endure, particularly their mental and emotional struggles. Beyond digital solutions, human connection, empathy, and a reliable support network are equally essential.

**What’s next?** It strengthened our determination to move forward with the initiative. We became more committed to developing practical solutions that respond to the needs of care receivers while fostering meaningful connections among patients, healthcare workers, volunteers, and communities. Ultimately, our goal was not simply to build a technological solution, but to contribute towards a more compassionate and supportive society where care receivers could live with greater comfort, dignity, and connection.
